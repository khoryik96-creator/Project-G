# Project G — Active Story Canon

This file records approved story rules that override older proposal language where they conflict.

## Core Leads

### Xu Kai

- Publicly appears to be a physically unimpressive civilian strategist.
- Solves problems through observation, psychology, manipulation, economics, social engineering, bluffing, timing, and unhinged but logical schemes.
- Secretly possesses overwhelming martial ability.
- Early chapters deliberately contain no reader-only martial hints, impossible reflexes, mysterious weapon breaks, hidden pressure, suspicious knockouts, or similar winks.
- Readers should have had a fair reason to believe Xu Kai genuinely relied on his mind because he lacked martial strength.
- He calls Jian Yue **“Oi.”**
- State records for Xu Kai begin only seven years before the current story.
- Before his legal identity was created, he already used the given name **Kai** but had no family name he could prove to the state.
- He chose **Xu** as his family name in memory of Doctor Xu, a physician who treated displaced children for free after the Heshan disasters.
- Xu Kai still does not know, or has not established, his original birth name or family record.
- Gao Wen's possible **Shen Kai** record is proven to be a recycled disaster-era identity belonging to a child who died twelve years earlier. Xu Kai is not established as Shen Kai.
- After the Revenue Ministry arc, Xu Kai becomes an indefinitely appointed **Imperial Examiner** working with the Five Pillars while remaining formally separate from the National-Tier ranking structure.
- Xu Kai's philosophy is now explicit: he hides his martial ability because once people know he can simply break the board, they stop asking whether the game itself is stupid. He genuinely prefers solving problems with his mind.
- His first undeniable martial reveal occurs in **Chapter 58**, privately during Jian Yue's rescue.
- At the reveal, he catches former Commander Wei Cang's saber between two fingers, snaps it, neutralizes Wei Cang's internal-force attack with a touch, and defeats him with a single light strike.
- The reveal establishes that Xu Kai is far beyond what Jian Yue assumed, but his exact ceiling remains unrevealed.
- In Chapter 63, Jian Yue tests him privately and cannot meaningfully touch him even after escalating to serious National-Tier speed. When she asks how he compares with Han Zhen, Xu Kai asks whether she truly wants the answer; she chooses not to hear it yet.

### Jian Yue

- National Tier #2 and one of the country's Five Pillars.
- Proud, sharp-tongued, highly competent, and tsundere.
- Assigned to supervise Xu Kai after the Qingshi incident.
- For the first half of the story she genuinely believes Xu Kai needs physical protection.
- Her repeated explanation for protecting, following, feeding, checking on, or otherwise caring about Xu Kai is that he is a **state security concern**.
- “Oi” begins as something she hates and gradually becomes shared private language.
- By the eastern frontier, Heshan, and Revenue Ministry arcs she clearly prioritizes Xu Kai personally, though she still refuses to frame this as romance.
- Xu Kai using her actual name should remain rare enough to feel meaningful.
- In Chapter 44 she jokingly calls Xu Kai **“Oi”** for the first time.
- During the Common Scale arc she is deliberately targeted because she is the most visible emergency responder among the Five.
- She is exposed to **black reed**, a meridian suppressant whose effect worsens with stronger internal-energy circulation, while rescuing civilians from a staged warehouse disaster.
- She is abducted and kept suppressed for the Common Scale's attempted seventy-two-hour demonstration.
- Xu Kai becomes the one protecting and rescuing her for the first time.
- Jian Yue is the first Pillar to learn Xu Kai's martial secret.
- After the reveal, her anger comes primarily from realizing she has sincerely been protecting someone monstrously stronger than he appeared.
- She immediately chooses to protect his secret from the other Four Pillars.
- Publicly she continues stepping in front of Xu Kai when danger appears, now partly to preserve his cover and partly because she refuses to surrender the habit.
- Privately, the power dynamic becomes more equal and more intimate because she now knows what he has trusted her with.

## Relationship Tone

- Dialogue-heavy.
- Banter before overt romance.
- Jian Yue's affection grows through behavior, protectiveness, jealousy, trust, and increasingly weak excuses rather than immediate confession.
- Xu Kai teases her reactions without becoming cruel.
- Jian Yue remains genuinely formidable and important; romance never reduces her to a damsel.
- The Common Scale rescue is the deliberate reversal of their established dynamic: Xu Kai finally steps in front of Jian Yue and tells her, **“For once, stay behind me.”**
- During the escape, Xu Kai admits plainly that he was scared when Jian Yue disappeared. This is one of his first emotionally direct admissions.
- After the rescue Jian Yue tells Xu Kai that when she protects him publicly he must continue playing along to preserve his cover.
- In Chapter 62, Xu Kai tells her, “No, you do not,” when she says she hates him. Jian Yue answers, “Good,” before immediately becoming embarrassed.
- Only Jian Yue currently knows enough to understand that Xu Kai's public weakness is a deliberate fiction.
- Lin Shuying can remain a playful jealousy catalyst, but not a serious love triangle unless explicitly changed later.

## The Five Pillars

- All five possess National-Tier strength.
- They are national protectors, not conventional villains.
- Their conflict with Xu Kai comes from legitimate security concerns, incompatible methods, incomplete information, and personality clashes.
- **#1 Han Zhen** — Grand Marshal; strongest officially recognized martial artist in the country; disciplined, severe, military-minded, and initially deeply distrustful of Xu Kai.
- **#2 Jian Yue** — sword specialist and female lead; Xu Kai's assigned supervisor and currently the only Pillar who knows his martial secret.
- **#3 Yan Shuo** — the Eastern Spear; frontier commander, blunt, decisive, practical, and now respectful of Xu Kai's strategic thinking.
- **#4 Lu Chen** — intelligence/security specialist; dry, observant, increasingly resigned to Xu Kai's methods, and suspicious that Xu Kai and Jian Yue are now jointly hiding something.
- **#5 Bai Ren** — elderly independent Pillar with a relaxed temperament and a sense of humor. He increasingly notices the relationship dynamics around the sixth chair.
- By Chapter 44 the symbolic **sixth chair** in the Hall of Five is moved permanently to the table for Xu Kai. This does not make him a Pillar or publicly National Tier; it marks that the Five now treat his strategic role as institutionally important.

## Political Arcs

### Blue Lantern / White Bridge

- Blue Lantern tracked the Five Pillars and measured national response times when they were absent.
- Its members believed overdependence on the Five had weakened provincial/local institutions.
- Their original demonstrations were designed to expose weakness without causing mass casualties.
- Minister Cao exploited the network and tried to turn a controlled White Bridge demonstration into a lethal incident.
- The plot was stopped without revealing any martial ability from Xu Kai.

### Eastern Frontier / Manufactured War

- Military supplies were siphoned through corrupt depot and prefectural systems, with false manifests creating the illusion that full convoys had departed and disappeared.
- False evidence and forged military orders were used to make Wuyuan and Liangbei believe the other side initiated attacks.
- Xu Kai exposed the fraud through logistics, civilian testimony, supply habits, market pricing, records, and social observation rather than combat.
- Liangbei representatives introduced in this arc include **Lady Lin Shuying**, **General Mu Qiao**, and intelligence officer **Mister Fan**.
- A battle was prevented when Xu Kai deliberately disrupted certainty and command signaling long enough for both armies to verify their orders.
- Yan Shuo grants Xu Kai an eastern command pass for future emergencies.

### Heshan / Missing Identity

- Lu Chen discovers that Xu Kai's legal existence begins seven years before the current story.
- Registrar **Sun Jiao** created legal identities for Xu Kai and eight other displaced adults whose original paperwork had been destroyed, lost, or never properly existed.
- Xu Kai previously lived at **Heshan Relief House**, a shelter/school/kitchen serving displaced people after major landslides and floods.
- Even then Xu Kai solved problems through observation and accounting: counting bowls, tracking lamp oil, comparing invoices, and noticing supply discrepancies.
- Heshan Relief House burned seven years ago. The official claim was a kitchen accident, but the fire began in the registry room.
- **Mei Rong**, another survivor, later became an Imperial Archive restorer and manipulated records to force the Heshan investigation to reopen.
- The old evidence ultimately points toward the Revenue Ministry corruption network rather than a hidden martial origin.

### Revenue Ministry / Gao Wen

- Gao Wen did **not** originally create the provincial relief-fraud system.
- He discovered it as a provincial deputy and chose managed corruption over institutional collapse.
- **Qiao Min**, former reconciliation accountant, discovered the dual-ledger system and was paid to disappear; the state later recorded him as dead twice while continuing to pay a pension under his identity.
- Xu Kai forces the Revenue Ministry into a supervised audit rather than making a narrow accusation. Departments begin producing evidence against one another to protect themselves.
- Gao Wen offers Xu Kai a possible original identity naming him **Shen Kai** in exchange for ending the Heshan investigation. Xu Kai refuses.
- Jian Yue explicitly tells Xu Kai that whether or not Shen is true, **Xu Kai is his name because he chose it**.
- Gao Wen resigns after identifying thirty-one officials, eleven contractors, four retired administrators, and one sitting governor connected to the network. He remains under restricted residence pending trial.
- Heshan Relief House receives a state memorial grant.

### Common Scale / Jian Yue Rescue

- **Qin Ruo** is a former disaster magistrate and reform advocate who argues that dependence on the Five Pillars has weakened local emergency institutions.
- The **Common Scale** begins as a loose reform circle of provincial administrators, physicians, engineers, and retired officers who want stronger institutions capable of functioning without immediate National-Tier intervention.
- Qin Ruo shares the movement's political argument but does not authorize Jian Yue's kidnapping.
- A radical cell led by former Commander **Wei Cang** stages a warehouse disaster designed specifically to draw Jian Yue into black-reed smoke while she rescues civilians.
- Black reed is nearly harmless to ordinary people but suppresses meridians increasingly severely as a martial artist circulates greater internal energy.
- Wei Cang's stated goal is to hold Jian Yue for seventy-two hours and prove the country can continue functioning without its most visible emergency responder.
- Xu Kai refuses a brute-force rescue because the cell prepared specifically for military and National-Tier responses.
- He tracks the kidnappers using black-reed purchasing patterns, bread routes, licensing disruptions, fake inspections, safe-house logistics, ration seals, and deliberate false rescue leaks.
- He intentionally gets himself captured to enter **Site Twenty-Seven** and bring a concealed tracking thread to Jian Yue.
- Once inside, Xu Kai fractures the Common Scale politically by forcing members to confront that the demonstration already succeeded and continued detention is simply kidnapping.
- Wei Cang loses control and changes the plan from demonstration to killing Jian Yue so the country must confront life without her.
- Xu Kai tries argument first and nearly talks him down.
- When Wei attacks Jian Yue anyway, Xu Kai makes his first undeniable martial reveal and defeats him effortlessly.
- Qin Ruo witnesses enough to understand something impossible happened and deliberately describes it as a “structural failure,” signaling that he will not immediately expose Xu Kai.
- Jian Yue lies to the other Four Pillars and claims she recovered enough internal energy to defeat Wei Cang herself.
- Lu Chen openly suspects that both Jian Yue and Xu Kai are lying but lacks proof.
- The government separates criminal responsibility from the movement's legitimate reform ideas instead of purging all Common Scale supporters.
- The resulting reforms create provincial emergency teams, redundant communications, local evacuation authority, and mandatory crisis exercises without Pillar participation.
- Qin Ruo chairs the civilian reform commission.
- Wei Cang remains alive in state custody pending trial.

## Martial Reveal Rules Going Forward

- The “no hint” phase is now complete as of Chapter 58.
- The public still does **not** know Xu Kai is martial at all.
- The other Four Pillars do **not** know his true strength.
- Jian Yue knows he is vastly stronger than he pretended, but she does not yet know his exact ceiling.
- Xu Kai should continue preferring wit. Martial strength must not become the default solution simply because the reader now knows it exists.
- When Xu Kai uses force, it should remain unusually efficient, understated, and often nameless rather than becoming a conventional technique-spam power fantasy.
- His exact rank should remain undefined. The fact that Jian Yue chooses not to hear a direct comparison with Han Zhen preserves the scale mystery.
- Xu Kai's unknown birth identity and martial past may now begin to intersect, but revelations should be gradual and should not retroactively invalidate the Heshan civilian history.

## New Martial-Past Hook

- Chapter 64 ends with a plain black envelope sealed in white wax addressed to Xu Kai.
- The message reads: **“TO THE MAN WHO ENDED BLACK RAIN IN ONE NIGHT: WE FOUND YOU.”**
- The Five do not know what Black Rain means.
- Xu Kai visibly recognizes the reference.
- Jian Yue understands that it is connected to the secret he revealed to her, but does not expose him.
- “Black Rain” is now the next major long-form mystery and can begin revealing Xu Kai's martial past while preserving his civilian life and strategic identity as equally real parts of him.
