# Project G — Active Story Canon

This file records approved story rules that override older proposal language where they conflict.

## Core Leads

### Xu Kai

- Publicly appears to be a physically unimpressive civilian strategist.
- Solves problems through observation, psychology, manipulation, economics, social engineering, bluffing, timing, and unhinged but logical schemes.
- Secretly possesses overwhelming martial ability, but this is **not to be indicated early**.
- Early chapters must not contain reader-only martial hints, impossible reflexes, mysterious weapon breaks, hidden pressure, suspicious knockouts, or similar winks.
- Readers should initially have a fair reason to believe Xu Kai genuinely relies on his mind because he lacks martial strength.
- His eventual martial reveal should therefore recontextualize the entire story rather than confirm an obvious early suspicion.
- He calls Jian Yue **“Oi.”**
- State records for Xu Kai begin only seven years before the current story.
- Before his legal identity was created, he already used the given name **Kai** but had no family name he could prove to the state.
- He chose **Xu** as his family name in memory of Doctor Xu, a physician who treated displaced children for free after the Heshan disasters.
- Xu Kai still does not know, or has not established, his original birth name or family record.
- His missing early identity must remain a personal/political mystery and must not be used as an early martial-power hint.

### Jian Yue

- National Tier #2 and one of the country's Five Pillars.
- Proud, sharp-tongued, highly competent, and tsundere.
- Assigned to supervise Xu Kai after the Qingshi incident.
- Genuinely believes Xu Kai needs physical protection.
- Her repeated explanation for protecting, following, feeding, checking on, or otherwise caring about Xu Kai is that he is a **state security concern**.
- “Oi” begins as something she hates and gradually becomes something only Xu Kai is allowed to call her.
- By the eastern frontier and Heshan arcs she clearly prioritizes Xu Kai personally, though she still refuses to frame this as romance.
- Xu Kai using her actual name should remain rare enough to feel meaningful.
- When Han Zhen considers temporary confinement during the identity investigation, Jian Yue opposes it immediately and personally.

## Relationship Tone

- Dialogue-heavy.
- Banter before overt romance.
- Jian Yue's affection should grow through behavior, protectiveness, jealousy, trust, and increasingly weak excuses rather than immediate confession.
- Xu Kai should tease her reactions without becoming cruel.
- Jian Yue remains genuinely formidable and important; romance never reduces her to a damsel.
- Their emotional progression should be visible through small choices rather than sudden confessions.
- Jian Yue increasingly trusts Xu Kai even when she does not know his full past.
- Lin Shuying can be used as a playful jealousy catalyst, but not as a serious love triangle unless explicitly changed later.

## The Five Pillars

- All five possess National-Tier strength.
- They are national protectors, not conventional villains.
- Their conflict with Xu Kai comes from legitimate security concerns, incompatible methods, incomplete information, and personality clashes.
- **#1 Han Zhen** — Grand Marshal; strongest officially recognized martial artist in the country; disciplined, severe, military-minded, and initially deeply distrustful of Xu Kai.
- **#2 Jian Yue** — Sword specialist and female lead; Xu Kai's assigned supervisor.
- **#3 Yan Shuo** — the Eastern Spear; frontier commander, blunt, decisive, practical, and initially skeptical of Xu Kai's indirect methods. The eastern frontier arc earns him genuine respect for Xu Kai's strategic thinking.
- **#4 Lu Chen** — intelligence/security specialist; dry, observant, and increasingly resigned to Xu Kai's methods. He leads the formal investigation into Xu Kai's identity gap.
- **#5 Bai Ren** — elderly independent Pillar with a relaxed temperament and a sense of humor; introduced during the Hall of Five meeting.

## Political Arcs

### Blue Lantern / White Bridge

- A network informally referred to as **Blue Lantern** tracked the Five Pillars and measured national response times when they were absent.
- Blue Lantern was not a conventional villain organization. Its members believed overdependence on the Five had weakened provincial/local institutions.
- Their original demonstrations were designed to expose that weakness without causing mass casualties.
- Minister Cao exploited the network and attempted to turn a controlled White Bridge demonstration into a lethal incident so provincial reformers would appear to be terrorists.
- The White Bridge plot was stopped without revealing any martial ability from Xu Kai.

### Eastern Frontier / Manufactured War

- Military supplies were siphoned through corrupt depot and prefectural systems, with false manifests creating the illusion that full convoys had departed and disappeared.
- The same network resold supplies, fabricated local military levies, and profited from tension along the Wuyuan–Liangbei frontier.
- False evidence and forged military orders were used to make both countries believe the other side had initiated attacks.
- Xu Kai helped expose the fraud through logistics, civilian testimony, supply habits, market pricing, records, and social observation rather than combat.
- Liangbei representatives introduced in this arc include **Lady Lin Shuying**, **General Mu Qiao**, and intelligence officer **Mister Fan**.
- A battle was prevented when Xu Kai deliberately disrupted certainty and command signaling long enough for both armies to verify their orders.
- Yan Shuo now trusts Xu Kai enough to grant him an eastern command pass for future emergencies.

### Heshan / Missing Identity

- Lu Chen discovers that Xu Kai's legal existence begins seven years before the current story.
- Registrar **Sun Jiao** created legal identities for Xu Kai and eight other displaced adults whose original paperwork had been destroyed, lost, or never properly existed.
- Sun Jiao was legally corrupt but morally more complicated than first assumed; he largely used the payments to process otherwise unrecognized displaced people into the state system.
- Xu Kai previously lived at **Heshan Relief House**, a shelter/school/kitchen serving displaced people after major landslides and floods.
- Even then Xu Kai solved problems through observation and accounting: counting bowls, tracking lamp oil, comparing invoices, and noticing supply discrepancies.
- Heshan Relief House burned seven years ago. The official claim was a kitchen accident, but the fire began in the registry room.
- The fire destroyed procurement records and evidence connected to missing relief grain, cloth, medicine, and duplicated invoices.
- **Gao Wen**, then connected to the Provincial Relief Office and now Vice Minister of Revenue / member of the Imperial Finance Council, is tied to the old procurement records.
- **Mei Rong**, another survivor whose legal identity was processed by Sun Jiao, later became an Imperial Archive restorer.
- Mei Rong planted a rebuilt Heshan census book and a deliberately imperfect forged Liangbei personnel file to force powerful institutions to investigate Xu Kai's missing identity and thereby reopen Heshan.
- Mei Rong's methods are reckless and ethically questionable, but her goal is to expose the buried Heshan corruption rather than harm Xu Kai.
- The supposed “first page” is actually an old procurement receipt showing missing grain and carrying Gao Wen's receiving seal. Xu Kai's own old notes on the reverse connect duplicated invoices to the days immediately before the Relief House fire.
- Gao Wen learns the Heshan investigation has reopened and issues an order demanding that all evidence be surrendered to him for internal review.

## Power Reveal Rule

**Do not reveal or imply Xu Kai's true martial power in the early story.**

When the first undeniable reveal eventually happens, it must be a major story event with emotional stakes. Until then, every dangerous situation involving Xu Kai should be resolved through his wit, another character's strength, planning, environmental exploitation, social leverage, or believable luck that does not imply supernatural ability.

His unknown birth identity, Heshan history, or missing records must **not** be used as coded evidence of martial training or secret strength.
