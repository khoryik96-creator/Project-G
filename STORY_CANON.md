# Project G — Active Story Canon

This file records approved story rules that override older proposal language where they conflict.

## Core Leads

### Xu Kai

- Publicly appears to be a physically unimpressive civilian strategist.
- Solves problems through observation, psychology, manipulation, economics, social engineering, bluffing, timing, and unhinged but logical schemes.
- Secretly possesses overwhelming martial ability.
- Early chapters deliberately contain no reader-only martial hints, impossible reflexes, mysterious weapon breaks, hidden pressure, suspicious knockouts, or similar winks.
- He calls Jian Yue **“Oi.”**
- State records for Xu Kai begin only seven years before the current story.
- Before his legal identity was created, he already used the given name **Kai** but had no family name he could prove to the state.
- He chose **Xu** as his family name in memory of Doctor Xu, a physician who treated displaced children for free after the Heshan disasters.
- Gao Wen's possible **Shen Kai** identity is disproven; that name belonged to a child who died twelve years earlier.
- After the Revenue Ministry arc, Xu Kai becomes an indefinitely appointed **Imperial Examiner** working with the Five Pillars while remaining formally outside the National-Tier ranking structure.
- Xu Kai hides his strength because once people know he can simply break the board, they stop asking whether the game itself is stupid. He genuinely prefers solving problems with his mind.
- His first undeniable martial reveal occurs privately in **Chapter 58**, during Jian Yue's rescue.
- He catches Wei Cang's saber between two fingers, snaps it, neutralizes an internal-force attack with a touch, and defeats Wei with a single light strike.
- His exact ceiling remains unrevealed.
- In Chapter 63 Jian Yue tests him privately and cannot meaningfully touch him even after escalating to serious National-Tier speed.
- When she asks how he compares with Han Zhen, Xu Kai asks whether she truly wants the answer; she chooses not to hear it yet.
- Black Rain proves that Xu Kai had already combined strategy and overwhelming martial power years earlier: he did not simply beat the threat physically; he dismantled a distributed assassination doctrine through misinformation, codes, logistics, and engineered doubt, using force only against the final Rain Guard.

### Jian Yue

- National Tier #2 and one of the country's Five Pillars.
- Proud, sharp-tongued, highly competent, and tsundere.
- Originally assigned to supervise Xu Kai after Qingshi.
- For the first half of the story she sincerely believes Xu Kai needs physical protection.
- Her repeated excuse for protecting, following, feeding, checking on, or otherwise caring about him is that he is a **state security concern**.
- “Oi” begins as something she hates and becomes shared private language.
- By the eastern frontier, Heshan, Revenue Ministry, Common Scale, and Black Rain arcs she clearly prioritizes Xu Kai personally while still resisting overt romantic language.
- Xu Kai using her actual name should remain rare enough to feel meaningful.
- Jian Yue is the first Pillar to learn Xu Kai's martial secret.
- She immediately chooses to protect that secret from the other Four Pillars.
- Publicly she continues stepping in front of Xu Kai when danger appears, partly from habit and partly to preserve his cover.
- Privately the relationship becomes more equal because she now knows he can protect her too.
- During Black Rain she becomes increasingly competent at lying for him. By Chapter 80 she smoothly hides the word **UNMEASURABLE** on Gu Zhaolin's old capability assessment before Lu Chen can see it.

## Relationship Tone

- Dialogue-heavy.
- Banter before overt romance.
- Jian Yue's affection grows through behavior, protectiveness, jealousy, trust, and increasingly weak excuses rather than immediate confession.
- Xu Kai teases her without cruelty.
- Jian Yue remains genuinely formidable; romance never reduces her to a damsel.
- The Common Scale rescue reverses their established dynamic: Xu Kai finally steps in front of Jian Yue and tells her, **“For once, stay behind me.”**
- During the escape Xu Kai admits plainly that he was scared when Jian Yue disappeared.
- In Chapter 62 Xu Kai tells her, “No, you do not,” when she says she hates him. Jian Yue answers, “Good,” before becoming embarrassed.
- During Black Rain the trust reverses again in a healthier way: when Jian Yue chases Jiang Tai, Xu Kai tells her not to; she answers **“Trust me.”** He does.
- Jian Yue later explains that Xu Kai asked her to trust him for months while she thought he was weak, so he can trust her for five minutes while she is not.
- Only Jian Yue currently understands Xu Kai's true martial absurdity.
- Lin Shuying can remain a playful jealousy catalyst, not a serious love triangle unless explicitly changed.

## The Five Pillars

- All five possess National-Tier strength.
- They are national protectors, not conventional villains.
- Their disagreements with Xu Kai come from legitimate security concerns, incompatible methods, incomplete information, and personality clashes.
- **#1 Han Zhen** — Grand Marshal; strongest officially recognized martial artist; disciplined, severe, military-minded, initially deeply distrustful of Xu Kai.
- **#2 Jian Yue** — sword specialist and female lead; Xu Kai's original supervisor and currently the only Pillar who knows his martial secret.
- **#3 Yan Shuo** — the Eastern Spear; frontier commander, blunt, decisive, practical, now genuinely respectful of Xu Kai's strategic thinking.
- **#4 Lu Chen** — intelligence/security specialist; dry, observant, and increasingly certain Xu Kai and Jian Yue are jointly hiding something.
- **#5 Bai Ren** — elderly independent Pillar with a relaxed temperament and sense of humor.
- By Chapter 44 the symbolic **sixth chair** in the Hall of Five is permanently assigned to Xu Kai. This does not make him publicly National Tier; it marks his strategic importance.

## Major Political Arcs

### Blue Lantern / White Bridge

- Blue Lantern tracked the Five Pillars and measured national response times when they were absent.
- Its members believed overdependence on the Five had weakened provincial/local institutions.
- Their original demonstrations were intended to expose weakness without causing mass casualties.
- Minister Cao exploited the network and tried to make provincial reformers look like terrorists.
- Xu Kai stopped the plot without revealing martial ability.

### Eastern Frontier / Manufactured War

- Corrupt officials siphoned military supplies, falsified manifests, and manufactured tension between Wuyuan and Liangbei.
- Forged orders made both countries believe the other had initiated attacks.
- Xu Kai exposed the scheme through logistics, civilian testimony, supply habits, market pricing, records, and social observation.
- Liangbei representatives include **Lady Lin Shuying**, **General Mu Qiao**, and **Mister Fan**.
- Xu Kai prevents a battle by disrupting command certainty long enough for both armies to verify orders.
- Yan Shuo grants Xu Kai an eastern command pass.

### Heshan / Missing Identity

- Lu Chen discovers that Xu Kai's legal existence begins seven years before the current story.
- Registrar **Sun Jiao** created legal identities for Xu Kai and eight other displaced adults whose original records were destroyed, lost, or never existed.
- Xu Kai previously lived at **Heshan Relief House**, a shelter/school/kitchen serving displaced people after major landslides and floods.
- Even then he solved problems through observation and accounting: counting bowls, tracking lamp oil, comparing invoices, and noticing supply discrepancies.
- Heshan Relief House burned seven years ago; the official kitchen-fire story was false, and the fire began in the registry room.
- **Mei Rong**, another survivor, later became an Imperial Archive restorer and manipulated records to force the buried Heshan corruption to reopen.
- The evidence points toward Revenue Ministry corruption rather than a hidden martial origin.

### Revenue Ministry / Gao Wen

- Gao Wen did not create the provincial relief-fraud system; he discovered it and chose managed corruption over institutional collapse.
- **Qiao Min**, former reconciliation accountant, discovered the dual-ledger system and was paid to disappear; the state later recorded him as dead twice while continuing to pay his pension.
- Xu Kai forces the Revenue Ministry into a supervised self-audit rather than making a narrow accusation.
- Gao Wen offers Xu Kai a possible **Shen Kai** identity in exchange for ending the investigation. Xu Kai refuses.
- Jian Yue tells him that whether or not Shen is true, **Xu Kai is his name because he chose it**.
- Gao Wen resigns after identifying the larger network and remains under restricted residence pending trial.
- Heshan Relief House receives a state memorial grant.

### Common Scale / Jian Yue Rescue

- **Qin Ruo** is a reform advocate who argues that dependence on the Five Pillars has weakened local emergency institutions.
- The **Common Scale** begins as a loose reform movement of provincial administrators, physicians, engineers, and retired officers.
- Qin Ruo shares the reform argument but does not authorize Jian Yue's kidnapping.
- Radical former Commander **Wei Cang** stages a disaster designed to draw Jian Yue into **black reed**, a meridian suppressant that affects stronger internal-energy circulation more severely.
- Jian Yue is abducted and kept suppressed for a planned seventy-two-hour demonstration.
- Xu Kai tracks the kidnappers using purchasing patterns, bread routes, licensing disruptions, fake inspections, safe-house logistics, ration seals, and deliberate false rescue leaks.
- He intentionally gets captured to enter **Site Twenty-Seven**.
- Xu Kai fractures the movement politically by forcing members to recognize that the demonstration already succeeded and continued detention is simply kidnapping.
- Wei Cang changes the plan to killing Jian Yue.
- Xu Kai tries argument first, then reveals his strength when Wei attacks her anyway.
- Qin Ruo witnesses enough to know something impossible happened and deliberately records it as a “structural failure.”
- Jian Yue lies to the other Four and claims she recovered enough internal energy to defeat Wei herself.
- The government adopts much of the legitimate reform program rather than purging the movement.
- Provincial emergency teams, redundant communications, local evacuation authority, and crisis exercises without Pillar participation are created.
- Qin Ruo chairs the civilian reform commission.

### Black Rain

- **Black Rain** was a secret contingency doctrine created by Director **Gu Zhaolin** and the now-defunct **Office of Special Measures**.
- It consisted of distributed cells, sealed target lists, independent confirmation codes, fallback signals, and local assassination teams intended to act if the state was compromised by foreign occupation.
- Gu corrupted the doctrine during a succession dispute and used it to remove political enemies.
- Seven years earlier, Xu Kai dismantled almost all active cells in one night primarily through forged confirmations, redirected couriers, conflicting orders, altered signs, false evacuations, and engineered doubt.
- A funeral troupe playing the wrong emergency hymn helped freeze multiple cells while they awaited verification.
- By the ninth bell, only Gu's twelve-person **Rain Guard** remained active.
- Xu Kai physically defeated the twelve without killing them.
- Gu was already dying from injuries inflicted by his own people after they discovered he had altered activation conditions.
- Xu Kai kept Gu alive long enough to obtain cancellation seals for forty-three remaining cells.
- **Ning Sui**, former Black Rain cipher clerk and courier coordinator, survives under a false death and reappears after legacy dead drops begin moving again.
- Black Rain originally had twenty-one Rain Guard designations: twelve active members and nine reserve names compartmentalized away from both Xu Kai and Ning Sui.
- **Qin Fei**, former Rain Guard deputy, survives and hides for years after reserve member **Jiang Tai / Reserve Eight** begins hunting old reserve survivors.
- Jiang Tai believes Black Rain failed because Gu built too much conscience into it: civilian-proximity cancellation, multiple confirmations, local delay authority, courier refusal, and hidden veto authorities.
- Jiang wants the second doctrine archive so he can rebuild a version without those safeguards.
- Xu Kai and the others locate the second archive inside an old Yunqiao flood-control system.
- Gu left a posthumous note admitting Black Rain was wrong and stating that he entrusted the master index to the one person he believed would never use it: Xu Kai.
- Gu hid the index as cipher silk inside a hollow brass rain-gauge token he gave Xu Kai seven years earlier.
- Xu Kai had accidentally kept the real token in a rice jar and carried a market-made fake for years; Jiang steals the fake and mistakes an old nine-person lunch receipt for a list of veto authorities.
- Nine completely ordinary former lunch companions therefore become accidental national assets for one night.
- Jiang's false-target operation exposes his reporting network.
- Jiang then uses Old Gate Twelve as a diversion while triggering false emergencies in three provinces, trying to prove Wuyuan still collapses whenever all Five Pillars gather in one place.
- Xu Kai refuses to let the Five respond personally and instead activates the Common Scale reforms: provincial response councils, local verification, and civilian emergency boards solve the alarms without National-Tier intervention.
- This defeats Jiang's political thesis before the final confrontation.
- Jiang attempts to force Xu Kai to reveal his martial strength publicly, but Jian Yue refuses to let him control the terms and fights Jiang herself.
- Jian Yue defeats and captures Jiang Tai without Xu Kai exposing his power to the other Four.
- Nine real living veto authorities then cancel Jiang's final Black Rain activation. Qin Fei discovers that Gu secretly made him veto authority nine.
- Black Rain is dismantled properly this time: documented, its surviving cells identified, its manuals sealed under independent custodians, and its cancellation authorities placed under civilian oversight.
- Qin Fei receives amnesty under protest. Ning Sui refuses government employment before anyone can offer it.
- Gu's surviving personnel assessment describes Xu Kai's principal danger not as capability but as his ability to make people question the order they were given.
- Beneath censor ink, Jian Yue sees the older capability judgment: **UNMEASURABLE**. She hides it from Lu Chen.

## Martial Reveal Rules Going Forward

- The “no hint” phase ended in Chapter 58.
- The public still does **not** know Xu Kai is martial at all.
- Han Zhen, Yan Shuo, Lu Chen, and Bai Ren do **not** know his true strength.
- Jian Yue knows he is vastly stronger than he pretended but still does not know his exact ceiling.
- Xu Kai should continue preferring wit. Martial strength must not become the default solution simply because the reader now knows it exists.
- When Xu Kai uses force, it should remain unusually efficient, understated, and often nameless rather than becoming conventional technique spam.
- His exact rank remains undefined.
- His unknown birth identity and martial past may intersect gradually, but revelations must not retroactively invalidate the Heshan civilian history.

## Current Hook

- Chapter 80 ends after Black Rain is permanently terminated.
- Liangbei sends a gold-edged diplomatic request asking specifically for **Imperial Examiner Xu Kai** to handle emergency treaty negotiations.
- Jian Yue insists that an already-promised dinner is also a matter of state.
- This opens a diplomacy-focused arc rather than another immediate hidden-martial conspiracy.
